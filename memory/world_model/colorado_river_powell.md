# Colorado River / Lake Powell — Situation Monitor

**Status: 🟠 SITUATION MONITOR — NOT A TRADE THESIS. The headline "dead pool → buy power" trade is DEAD ON ARRIVAL: the affected utilities are almost entirely non-tradeable (munis, rural co-ops, tribal utilities), the lost generation is ~1% of the regional supply picture, and the decline has been forecast monthly by a federal agency since 2021. Zero temporal edge. This file exists to track the WATER-DELIVERY constraint (the actually-underpriced channel) and the Oct 1, 2026 guidelines catalyst.**
**As of: August 7, 2026 (v1 — seeded)**

## What happened (verified)
- Lake Powell at **~3,521.6 ft, ~22–23% of capacity** (Aug 5–7, 2026); ~5.55 MAF against ~24 MAF design capacity. Combined Powell+Mead storage (~12.40 MAF) is the **lowest on record**.
- **31–34 ft above minimum power pool (3,490 ft)** — the elevation at which Glen Canyon's turbines must shut down to avoid cavitation.
- 2026 inflow was catastrophic: lower-probable forecast of **2.78 MAF = 29% of historical average**, one of the lowest on record. A May forecast briefly showed 13% of usual flows.
- Glen Canyon: 8 generators, **1,320 MW**, ~5 billion kWh/yr typical, **73–85% of all CRSP generation**. Sold to ~130 wholesale customers serving ~5M people and 50+ tribal nations across AZ/UT/CO/NM/WY/NV/NE.
- WAPA is already underwater on replacement power: it paid **more per MWh than it charges customers every year FY2023–25** — roughly tripled its rate in 2023, narrowing to ~35% above by 2025. Separately spent $18.9M (2024) and $6.5M (2025) on "Cool Mix" replacement power.

## ⚠️ The premise correction (read this first)
**The situation IMPROVED versus the April scare.** Reclamation's April 24-Month Study projected crossing minimum power pool by **August 2026**. Current projection is **March 2027**, and the decline has leveled off running ~2 ft above forecast.

But note *how* that reprieve was bought: Interior authorized **smaller releases from Powell**. The water wasn't found — it was withheld from downstream. Powell's turbines were protected by pushing the shortfall onto Lake Mead and the Lower Basin. Anyone reading an alarm about this today is reading a fear that peaked four months ago, while the actual transferred risk moved somewhere the headline doesn't point.

## Why the obvious trade fails
1. **The victims aren't listed.** CRSP firm-power customers are municipal utilities, rural electric co-ops, and tribal utilities. There is no clean equity expressing "CRSP customers pay more."
2. **Scale mismatch.** 1,320 MW nameplate against a region where Arizona's governor is planning for **up to 29,000 MW** of new data-center-driven load and Utah has 2,600 MW under construction. Glen Canyon is a rounding error against the demand wave, not the story.
3. **Maximally telegraphed.** Reclamation publishes 24-Month Studies *monthly*. This is the most-forecast slow-motion infrastructure event in the United States. "Inevitable" here is a warning, not a green light — when everyone can see it and a federal agency publishes the date every month, the information is ambience, not edge.
4. **The gas-turbine tightness is real but not caused by this.** Large gas turbines are sold out through 2030 — driven by data centers. Glen Canyon adds ~1% to that. It is a confirming detail in an already-crowded trade, not an originating one.

## The actually non-obvious channel: plumbing, not electricity
Below minimum power pool, releases can only pass through the **river outlet works** — four 96-inch pipes, combined max **15,000 cfs at elevation 3,500 ft, declining below that**.

At **3,430 ft, ROW capacity is ~10,200 cfs ≈ 7.41 MAF/yr — against the Law of the River's 8.23 MAF/yr Lower Basin obligation.**

That is the buried point. Below a certain elevation, the delivery shortfall stops being an *allocation* question and becomes a **physical throughput** question: Reclamation cannot pass the required water even where the law commands it. The ROWs also have a known design flaw — unsafe for extended use, and they erode when the reservoir is low. A compact-obligation failure caused by plumbing rather than policy is the trigger for interstate litigation that resolves over *years* in the Supreme Court, not over a quarter in a price move.

## The one dated catalyst
**October 1, 2026 (~8 weeks out).** The 2007 Interim Guidelines and 2019 Drought Contingency Plans **expire at the end of 2026**. Interior has moved forward with the Post-2026 NEPA process **without seven-state consensus**, targeting a Record of Decision by Oct 1. Lower Basin states submitted a joint proposal May 1; Upper and Lower Basins remain unaligned. As of Aug 2026, California is reportedly positioned for deeper cuts precisely because the states failed to agree.

Unlike the reservoir elevation, this is a **discrete, dated, publishable event**. If a catalyst exists in this complex, it is here — not at 3,490 ft.

## Counterintuitive: seniority appreciates
Intuition says water cuts hurt Colorado River agriculture. Priority law says the opposite for the seniors:
- **California holds the most senior mainstem rights**; IID + Coachella sit at third priority within California's Seven Party Agreement. IID is the single largest user on the river.
- Under prior appropriation, juniors are curtailed first. **Central Arizona Project (CAP) holds the junior position and absorbs cuts before IID gives up an acre-foot.**
- IID's participation in conservation has been **voluntary and explicitly structured to protect its senior rights** — up to 700,000 AF left in Mead through 2026, federally funded up to 300,000 AF/yr 2024–26, plus paid summer fallowing of forage/alfalfa with 80%+ of eligible acreage enrolled.

So scarcity converts senior entitlements into a **paid revenue stream** (fallowing compensation), not a loss. The correct directional read on the seniors is the inverse of the headline. **Key fragility:** the federal money funding those payments is drawing down, and post-2026 funding is unclear. Watch whether voluntary-and-paid becomes mandatory-and-unpaid — that is the regime change that would actually reprice things.
